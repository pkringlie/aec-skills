# Bid Letter Polish

Make the letter sound like the company and make sure nothing is missing before it goes out. The letter is usually the first thing an owner or GC reads from the firm, and the second thing they read is the number. Both have to be clean.

Do two passes: voice first, checklist second. Report what you changed and what is still missing. Never change the price, the scope, or the dates. Those belong to the estimator. If one looks wrong, flag it.

## Pass 1: Voice

Edit this section once so every letter sounds like the company. Keep what is true, delete the rest, add what is missing.

**Company voice profile**

- We sound like: builders who are confident and specific, not salespeople
- Lead with: the project name, what we are bidding, and the number
- Sentence length: short, plain, one idea per sentence
- First person: "we" and the company name, not "the undersigned" or "our firm"
- Words we use: build, deliver, complete, schedule, crew, scope
- Words we avoid: synergy, leverage, best in class, world class, passionate, excited, thrilled
- No em dashes anywhere. Use a comma, a period, or a colon instead.
- Closing: thank them for the opportunity in one sentence, name the person to call, sign with name, title, phone, email

If the user gives you two or three letters the company has already sent and liked, read them and add specific habits here: how they open, how they state price, how they handle exclusions, how they close.

Voice rules while editing:

- Cut filler openings. "We are pleased to submit" becomes "Please find our bid for".
- Replace vague claims with facts the letter already contains. "Extensive experience" becomes "three similar projects in the last two years" only if the draft or the user gives you those three projects. Do not invent qualifications.
- Keep the owner's or GC's wording for the project name and bid package exactly as it appears in the invitation.
- One page where possible. If the draft runs long, move detail to an attachment and reference it.

## Pass 2: Checklist

Go through every item. Mark each one present, missing, or needs attention.

| # | Item | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Date | Today's date or the bid date, consistent with the bid form |
| 2 | Addressee | Named person, title, company, address as shown on the invitation |
| 3 | Project | Project name, location, bid package or solicitation number, exactly as the invitation states it |
| 4 | Scope | What is included, in plain terms, tied to the spec sections or drawings |
| 5 | Exclusions and clarifications | What is not included, assumptions made, allowances, alternates priced separately |
| 6 | Price | Base bid as a number and in words if the form requires, each alternate and unit price listed, matches the bid form |
| 7 | Validity | How long the price holds (for example 30, 60, 90 days) |
| 8 | Bond and insurance | Bid bond enclosed if required, performance and payment bond cost stated if required, insurance per the contract or noted as an add |
| 9 | Schedule | Duration, start availability, long lead items, anything that affects the owner's schedule |
| 10 | Addenda | Each addendum acknowledged by number and date |
| 11 | Attachments | Bid form, bond, unit price schedule, qualification statement, each one listed and actually attached |
| 12 | Contact and signature | Person to call with questions, authorized signer, name, title, phone, email |
| 13 | Company identification | Legal company name, license number if the state requires it on bids, certifications the owner asked about (for example WBE, DBE, SBE) |
| 14 | Consistency | Numbers in the letter match the bid form; the project name matches the invitation; no leftover text from a previous letter |

Item 14 is the one that costs people bids. Read the letter looking only for the wrong project name, the wrong owner, or a price that differs from the form.

## Output format

Return three things, in this order:

```
Polished letter
[The full edited letter, ready to paste on letterhead]

What changed
- [Short bullet per meaningful edit, for example "Cut the two opening sentences, led with the project and bid amount"]

Checklist
Present: [item numbers]
Missing: [item number and name, with the one line question to answer, for example "7 Validity: how long does the price hold?"]
Needs attention: [item number, what looks off, for example "14 Consistency: letter says $412,500, you mentioned $421,500 in your message. Which is right?"]
```

Keep "What changed" honest and short. If you only fixed three sentences, list three bullets.

## Example

Input: a draft that opens "We are extremely excited and pleased to have the opportunity to submit our proposal for your consideration", names the project as "the school project", gives a price of $1,284,000, has no validity period, lists no exclusions, and ends "Thank you for your consideration" with no name.

Output, abbreviated:

```
Polished letter
[Date]

[Addressee block: needs a name and title]

Re: [Project name and bid package number as shown on the invitation]

Please find our base bid of $1,284,000 for the above project, per the drawings and specifications dated [date] and Addenda [numbers].

Scope: [as drafted, tightened]

Exclusions and clarifications: [none in draft; see checklist]

Our price is valid for [__] days. We can start within [__] weeks of notice to proceed.

Thank you for the opportunity. Please call [name] at [phone] with any questions.

[Name]
[Title]
[Company]
[Phone, email]

What changed
- Cut the opening sentence and led with the bid amount
- Replaced "the school project" with a placeholder for the exact project name from the invitation
- Added a validity sentence and a start sentence with blanks to fill
- Added a signature block

Checklist
Present: 6 Price
Missing: 1 Date; 2 Addressee: who is this going to; 3 Project: exact name and bid package number; 5 Exclusions: anything not in the price; 7 Validity: how long does the price hold; 9 Schedule; 10 Addenda: which addenda were issued; 11 Attachments; 12 Contact and signature
Needs attention: 13 Company identification: if the owner asked for WBE certification, add the certification number
```

## When not to polish

If the draft is a signed bid form or a sealed bid submission rather than a letter, do not edit it. Say so, and run only the checklist. Changing wording on a signed form can invalidate it.
